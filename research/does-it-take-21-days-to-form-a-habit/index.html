Does it take 21 days to form a habit?

By Nitish Vaibhav · Last reviewed 19 Nov 2026 · Educational, not medical advice

The short answer

No. The "21 days" idea comes from a 1960 self-help book, not a habit study. When UCL researchers tracked 96 people building a new daily habit, it took 66 days on average to feel automatic, ranging from 18 to an estimated 254 days. Missing a single day didn't undo progress.

Where does the 21-day rule come from?

It traces back to Psycho-Cybernetics (1960), a self-help book by plastic surgeon Maxwell Maltz. He observed that patients seemed to need "a minimum of about 21 days" to get used to a change, such as a new face after surgery. That was an observation about adjusting to change, not a measurement of habit formation, and over the decades "a minimum of about 21 days" became "21 days".

What did the actual habit study find?

In 2010, researchers at University College London asked 96 volunteers to pick one new daily behaviour tied to a cue, such as drinking a glass of water after breakfast or going for a run before dinner, and to rate how automatic it felt each day for 12 weeks.

  • Average time to automatic: 66 days (the median).
  • Range: 18 to about 254 days. The longest times were estimated from each person's curve, because the study ended at 84 days.
  • Harder behaviours took longer. Drinking water became automatic faster than doing 50 sit-ups.
  • Missing one day didn't reset progress.

Lally P et al., European Journal of Social Psychology, 2010

Why does the myth matter?

If you expect a habit to stick in three weeks, day 22 can feel like failure. Most people are only partway up the curve at that point. Planning for about two months is closer to what the research shows, and knowing a missed day is fine removes the "I broke the streak, so I quit" trap.

How do you build a habit faster?

  • Tie it to a cue you already have: "after I brush my teeth", "after I park the car".
  • Start small. Simpler behaviours become automatic sooner. Two minutes daily beats an hour now and then.
  • Same time, same place. Consistent context is what turns a behaviour automatic.
  • Miss a day? Just continue. One miss didn't affect the curve in the study; quitting does.

Frequently asked questions

How many days does it take to form a habit?

In the best-known study, 66 days on average, with a range from 18 to about 254 days depending on the person and the behaviour.

Is the 21-day rule true?

No. It comes from a 1960 self-help book's observation about adjusting to change, not from a study measuring habits.

Does missing a day ruin a habit?

No. In the UCL study, missing one opportunity didn't materially affect how the habit formed.
