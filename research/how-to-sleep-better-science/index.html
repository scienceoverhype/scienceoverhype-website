How can you sleep better? What the research supports

By Nitish Vaibhav · Last reviewed 19 Nov 2026 · Educational, not medical advice

The short answer

Most adults need 7 or more hours. Studies link short sleep to more hunger, a higher chance of catching a cold and slower thinking, often without feeling very tired. The habits with real evidence are simple: wake up at the same time daily, get morning daylight, stop caffeine 6 hours before bed and keep the bedroom cool and dark.

Is 6 hours of sleep enough?

For most adults, no. Sleep experts recommend 7 or more hours a night. The tricky part is that short sleep doesn't always feel bad:

  • Hunger goes up. After two nights of 4 hours in bed, healthy young men had lower leptin (the fullness hormone), higher ghrelin (the hunger hormone) and reported more hunger. Spiegel et al., Annals of Internal Medicine, 2004
  • You catch colds more easily. When 164 adults were given a cold virus, those who had slept under 6 hours a night were about 4 times more likely to get sick than those who slept over 7. Prather et al., Sleep, 2015
  • Focus drops, but you don't notice. After two weeks of 6 hours a night, people's attention fell to the level seen after a full night without sleep, while they rated themselves only slightly sleepy. Van Dongen et al., Sleep, 2003

Which sleep habits have real evidence?

  1. Wake up at the same time every day, including weekends. Your body clock runs on routine, and a fixed wake time anchors it.
  2. Get daylight in the morning. Morning light is the strongest signal that sets the body clock; evening light pushes it later.
  3. No caffeine in the 6 hours before bed. In a sleep study, caffeine taken 6 hours before bedtime still cut sleep by more than an hour. The dose was 400 mg, about 3–4 cups of coffee. If you sleep at 11 pm, make 5 pm your last chai or coffee. Drake et al., Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine, 2013
  4. Keep the room cool, dark and quiet, and the phone away from the bed.

What helps with a racing mind at night?

Write tomorrow's to-do list. In a sleep-lab study, people who spent 5 minutes writing a specific to-do list before bed fell asleep about 9 minutes faster than people who wrote about tasks they had already finished. Scullin et al., Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 2018

When should you see a doctor?

If you snore loudly, stop breathing in your sleep, wake up gasping, or feel very sleepy in the day despite enough time in bed, talk to a doctor. Conditions such as sleep apnoea need treatment, not tips.

Frequently asked questions

How many hours of sleep do adults need?

Sleep experts recommend 7 or more hours a night for adults.

How late can I drink coffee or chai?

Aim for no caffeine in the 6 hours before bed. In one study, caffeine 6 hours before bedtime still cut sleep by more than an hour.

Does writing a to-do list help you fall asleep?

In one sleep-lab study, people who wrote a specific to-do list for 5 minutes before bed fell asleep about 9 minutes faster.

Can you catch up on sleep at the weekend?

Sleeping in shifts your body clock. A fixed wake time every day is one of the most reliable ways to sleep better.
